# Admiralty, carriage of goods, customs and international trade

| Question | Facts and authorities to verify |
|---|---|
| Can the claimant arrest this ship? | Classify the claim under the Admiralty Act 1973, s 4, then identify the basis for an action in rem under s 5. For claims within s 5(2)(b), establish who would be personally liable and that person's relationship to the vessel when the claim arose. At filing, check beneficial ownership of all shares in the target vessel or a charter by demise to that person. Vessel names, fleet branding and group accounts can obscure the legal owner. Check High Court Rules 2016, Part 25, including r 25.34 on applying for a warrant. [Admiralty Act, ss 4–5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1973/0119/latest/whole.html) · [High Court Rules, Part 25](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2016/225/en/latest/) |
| Which terms govern damaged sea freight? | Obtain the bill of lading or sea waybill, charterparty, booking, loading and discharge ports, survey, delivery records and any extension agreement. Under the Maritime Transport Act 1994, ss 208–210 and Schedule 5, check the Rules' application, including Article 10 and any express incorporation in a non-negotiable document. Article 3(6) addresses notice, the evidential effect of removal, and the one-year period for suit measured from delivery or the due delivery date. Check the forum provision and the carrier's defences and limits before estimating recovery. [Maritime Transport Act, Part 16 and Schedule 5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/104/en/latest/) · [Text of the carriage rules](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/104/en/latest/sections/DLM334660/DLM339908) |
| Was the loss on a road or air leg? | Identify the contracting carrier, actual carriers, handover receipts and the route covered by each contract. For domestic freight within the Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017, Part 5 subpart 1, check ss 242–243 for scope, the agreed risk category, s 259 on limits, and ss 274–281 on notice and suit. For international air cargo, check which convention applies to the agreed route. Where the Montreal Convention governs, consult Arts 18, 22, 31 and 35 on cargo damage, limits, complaint and suit. [Domestic carriage regime](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/0005/latest/DLM6844598.html) · [Civil Aviation Act 2023, Schedule 6](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2023/10/en/latest/sections/LMS50416/) |
| Who must report a maritime incident? | Identify the flag, location, vessel operator and master, injuries and the type of event. Check the Maritime Transport Act 1994, s 31, and, for a workplace event, the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 and Maritime NZ's designated responsibilities. Record when the event became known, who notified Maritime NZ and whether local navigation bylaws call for a further notification. A harbourmaster report and a cargo insurance claim serve different purposes. [Maritime NZ notification guidance](https://maritimenz.govt.nz/about-us/notifying-maritime-nz/) |
| Which tariff, preference or permit applies? | Identify the product and composition, Customs value, tariff heading and version effective on import. For a preferential rate, check the agreement, product-specific rule of origin and supporting records. Separately identify applicable MPI import health standards for goods and packaging. For exports, check MFAT controls, the destination, end use and permits effective on shipment. [Customs Working Tariff Document](https://www.customs.govt.nz/business/tariffs/working-tariff-document) · [Preferential tariff rates](https://www.customs.govt.nz/business/import/preferential-tariff-duty-rates) · [MPI import health standards](https://www.mpi.govt.nz/import/importing-into-nz-how-it-works/import-health-standards) · [MFAT export controls](https://www.mfat.govt.nz/en/trade/export-controls/which-exports-are-controlled) |
| How can an importer dispute a Customs decision? | Record the decision type, notice date, reasons and statutory provision. For decisions eligible for both routes, the duty payer may seek an administrative review by Customs or appeal directly to the Customs Appeal Authority. The ordinary period is 20 working days from the date of the decision or assessment. For more time, check the extension procedure within that period. An administrative review determination can itself be appealed. [Customs eligibility table, deadlines and applications](https://www.customs.govt.nz/business/doing-business-with-customs/reviews-and-appeals) |

**Citation verification example.** An importer finds concealed damage four days after taking delivery and the carrier says the claim expired after three days. If the Schedule 5 Rules govern, Article 3(6) gives late notice an evidential consequence. Removal without timely notice is prima facie evidence of delivery as described. Check for a joint survey and seek photographs, temperature logs and handover records that might rebut that inference. Calculate the separate one-year deadline for suit and check whether the parties agreed to extend it after the cause of action arose. [Maritime Transport Act, Schedule 5, Article 3(6)](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/104/en/latest/sections/DLM334660/DLM339908)
