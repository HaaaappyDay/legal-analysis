# Te Tiriti o Waitangi, tikanga Māori, land and resource use

| Question | Decisive facts and authorities |
|---|---|
| What Treaty obligation applies to a Crown decision? | Read the Māori and English texts of Te Tiriti o Waitangi / the Treaty of Waitangi and the clause governing the particular decision. For example, State-Owned Enterprises Act 1986 s 9 constrains Crown action under that Act. For a Waitangi Tribunal claim, identify the Crown act or omission, prejudice, claimant and dates under ss 6 and 6AA. Most recommendations are non-binding. Sections 8A–8I contain special regimes for specified land. [Treaty of Waitangi Act 1975, ss 6–8I and sch 1](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1975/114/en/latest/) · [State-Owned Enterprises Act, s 9](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/124/en/latest/) |
| Who can sell or lease this block? | Use the legal description to find the Māori Land Court's status, ownership, succession and trust orders. Section 129 distinguishes Māori customary land, Māori freehold land and other statuses, and s 131 provides for status orders. For Māori freehold land, identify whether the proposed signatory is an owner, trustee or incorporation, then check the governing order, owner approval, confirmation or recording requirements for that transaction under Parts 7–8. [Te Ture Whenua Maori Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/4/en/latest/) |
| What does a Treaty settlement give the iwi at this site? | Match the claimant group and mapped site to the deed, settlement Act and any vesting or acknowledgement provision. For example, the Ngāi Tahu statutory acknowledgement for the Waiau records an association that can be cited under s 211. Section 219 limits its effect on property rights. A claim of ownership requires a vesting provision, title or other authority covering the parcel. [Ngāi Tahu Claims Settlement Act 1998, sch 69](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1998/97/en/latest/sections/DLM431302/) · [Section 219](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1998/97/en/latest/sections/DLM430058/) |
| What rights have been recognised on the coast? | Identify the group, activity and mapped area in the recognition order or agreement. Protected customary rights and customary marine title have different tests and consequences. For title, check ss 9A–9C, 57A–58 and sch 1AA before applying a decision made under the earlier test. [Marine and Coastal Area (Takutai Moana) Act 2011](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2011/3/en/latest/) |
| Which rules apply to a development or water take during planning reform? | Record the location, activity, lodging date and relevant transition date. Under Planning Act 2026 sch 1 cl 18, a pending pre-transition resource consent application continues under the Resource Management Act 1991 (RMA) as it stood immediately before royal assent. Clause 19 applies the RMA in force at lodgement to an RMA application lodged during transition. Check local plan rules for land use, water and discharge and the terms of any consent. For later decisions, identify the applicable planning consent or natural resource permit and how existing consents carry over under cls 33–37. [Planning Act 2026, sch 1 cl 18](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2026/70/en/latest/sections/LMS1628031/) · [Full Act, including cls 19 and 33–37](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2026/70/en/latest/) · [RMA, ss 9, 14–15](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1991/0069/latest/DLM4119181.html) · [Natural Environment Act 2026](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2026/71/en/latest/) |
| Can the operator start mining on private land? | Examine the minerals permit, access rights for each affected parcel under s 47 and following sections, any resource consent, and operating conditions. If the project was listed or referred for fast-track approval, obtain the panel's decision on each approval sought. [Crown Minerals Act 1991](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1991/70/en/latest/) · [Fast-track Approvals Act 2024, s 81](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2024/56/en/latest/sections/LMS978159/) |

**How does a court ascertain the relevant tikanga?** In *Ellis v R*, the Supreme Court considered whether a criminal appeal could continue after the appellant's death. The Court agreed that tikanga informs the development of the common law where relevant. The majority addressed how the relationship develops case by case and how a court can ascertain the relevant tikanga. In a land dispute, identify the iwi or hapū, the practice engaged, and evidence bearing on that place and dispute before drawing a conclusion about its effect. [*Ellis v R* [2022] NZSC 114 at [16]–[23]](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2022/2022-NZSC-114.pdf)
