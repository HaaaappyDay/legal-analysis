# Constitutional powers, official information and public rights

| Question | Decisive facts and authorities |
|---|---|
| Can a minister or council change this rule? | Identify who made the instrument. Parliament can enact legislation, and a bill becomes law on royal assent. Check commencement before applying it. For a regulation, bylaw or executive decision, identify the enabling provision, the permitted subject matter and any required consultation or approval. A minister's announcement alone does not establish that a new rule has taken effect. [Constitution Act 1986, ss 14–16](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/114/en/latest/) |
| Can a department withhold a report, or a council exclude the public from a meeting? | Identify who holds the information. Under the Official Information Act 1982, s 5 favours availability, s 6 specifies conclusive grounds, and s 9 requires consideration of the public interest in release. For a local authority, use the corresponding ss 5–7 of the Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act 1987. For a closed council meeting, obtain the resolution and the ground relied on under ss 47–48. A later request for the minutes requires its own withholding decision. [Official Information Act](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1982/156/en/latest/) · [Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1987/174/en/latest/) |
| Has the agency missed its response deadline? | Record receipt, any transfer or extension, the decision notice and the date of release. The ordinary outer limit of 20 working days applies to deciding and notifying the requester. The agency must act sooner where reasonably practicable. A grant followed by undue delay in release can also be taken to an Ombudsman. [Official Information Act, ss 14–15A, 28](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1982/156/en/latest/) · [Local Government Official Information and Meetings Act, ss 12–14, 27](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1987/174/en/latest/) |
| Can the High Court set aside a public decision? | Identify the statutory power, the reasons and record, and the alleged error. For an unfair hearing, specify what notice, material or opportunity to respond was missing. Section 27 of the Bill of Rights protects natural justice and access to judicial review in the circumstances it specifies. Check the enabling Act for appeal rights or special limits. On review, the High Court can grant interim relief, set a decision aside or direct reconsideration. [Judicial Review Procedure Act 2016, ss 5, 8, 15–18](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/50/en/latest/) · [New Zealand Bill of Rights Act 1990, s 27](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1990/109/en/latest/sections/DLM225529/) |
| Does discriminatory treatment support a claim? | For government conduct, check Bill of Rights ss 3, 5 and 19 and Human Rights Act Part 1A. For an employer or service provider, match the ground in s 21 to the activity in s 22 or s 44 and check exceptions. A complaint to the Human Rights Commission and civil proceedings before the Human Rights Review Tribunal serve different purposes. Section 92B specifies who may bring proceedings after a complaint. [Bill of Rights](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1990/109/en/latest/) · [Human Rights Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/82/en/latest/sections/DLM304921/) |

**Did a declaration of inconsistency change the voting age?** In *Make It 16*, the Supreme Court declared the minimum age of 18 for parliamentary and local elections inconsistent with the right to freedom from age discrimination. Its order did not set a new voting age. Sections 4–6 of the Bill of Rights govern the application and interpretation of enactments. Sections 7A–7B govern parliamentary notification and the government response to a final declaration. For eligibility at a particular election, check the electoral statute in force on the relevant date. [*Make It 16 Inc v Attorney-General* [2022] NZSC 134 at [69]–[72]](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2022/2022-NZSC-134.pdf) · [Bill of Rights](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1990/109/en/latest/)
