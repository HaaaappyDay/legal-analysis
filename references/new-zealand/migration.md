# Immigration, refugee protection and citizenship

| Question | Decisive facts and authorities |
|---|---|
| Does a visa allow this job or change of employer? | Record the visa class, grant, expiry, conditions and any approved variation. Check the relevant clause and effective date of the certified immigration instructions issued under Immigration Act 2009 s 22. Section 52 provides for imposing and varying conditions on the temporary visas it covers. Compare the person's actual conditions with the proposed employer, occupation, location and dates. [Immigration Act, s 52](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/51/en/2026-07-10/sections/DLM1440656/) · [Immigration New Zealand's Operational Manual and amendment circulars](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/about-us/immigration-policy-and-law/immigration-instructions-and-changes/immigration-instructions/) |
| Can a visa refusal be reconsidered or appealed? | Section 185 covers an onshore holder who applied for a further temporary visa while the original visa was current and received a refusal from someone other than the Minister. Under s 185(2), the applicant must still be lawfully in New Zealand when seeking reconsideration and apply within 14 days of receiving notice. For a residence-class decision, establish whether s 187 confers an appeal to the Immigration and Protection Tribunal. Section 188 measures correctness against the residence instructions applicable when the visa application was made. [Immigration Act, s 185](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/51/en/latest/sections/DLM1440890/) · [Immigration Act, s 188](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/51/en/latest/sections/DLM1440896/) |
| Would return expose the claimant to persecution, torture or another protected harm? | Apply the Convention refugee definition under s 129, including the link between feared persecution and a Convention ground. Separately assess danger of torture under s 130 and arbitrary deprivation of life or cruel treatment under s 131. Relate country evidence to the claimant and identify the meaningful domestic protection available to them. For an adverse determination, distinguish a first claim under s 194 from a subsequent claim under s 195, and check notification and detention status when calculating time. [Immigration Act, Part 5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/0051/latest/DLM1441228.html) · [Immigration Act, Part 7](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/51/en/latest/) |
| How can deportation or a Tribunal decision be challenged? | Identify the basis of deportation liability and the appeal available. For a humanitarian appeal under ss 206–207, address the exceptional circumstances, why deportation would be unjust or unduly harsh, and whether allowing the person to remain would be contrary to the public interest. For a challenge in court, distinguish leave to appeal on a question of law (s 245) from judicial review and its restrictions (ss 247–249). Check which decision started time running. [Immigration Act, ss 206–207](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/0051/latest/DLM1441042.html) · [Court proceedings](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2009/0051/latest/DLM1440695.html) |
| Is the person already a citizen, or must they apply for a grant? | For birth in New Zealand on or after 1 January 2006, check parental citizenship or qualifying indefinite residence at birth and the exceptions in s 6, including statelessness. For birth overseas, examine descent under s 7. For an ordinary adult grant under s 8, verify entitlement to stay indefinitely, qualifying presence of 1,350 days in the preceding five years and 240 days in each of those years, and the remaining statutory criteria. Check any applicable statutory discretion against the applicant's circumstances. [Citizenship Act 1977, ss 6–9](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1977/61/en/latest/) |

**Does climate-related displacement itself establish refugee or protected-person status?** In *Teitiota*, the Supreme Court declined leave to appeal on the evidence of the applicant's circumstances in Kiribati and the measures taken there. It left open the possibility that environmental degradation could engage the refugee or protection jurisdiction on different facts. Identify the alleged harm on return, the evidence that it threatens this claimant, and the statutory route under which it matters. [*Teitiota v Chief Executive of the Ministry of Business, Innovation and Employment* [2015] NZSC 107 at [12]–[14]](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2015/2015-NZSC-107.pdf)
