# Contracts, consumer guarantees and fair trading

Identify the parties, when the contract was made, what each promised, how the goods or services were supplied, and the remedy sought. For a marketplace purchase, establish who sold the item and whether that person acted in trade. A private sale, a trader's sale of second-hand goods and a manufacturer's warranty raise different questions.

| Proposition to check | Questions and authorities |
|---|---|
| A term binds the parties | What offer was accepted, and when? Which terms had the buyer seen by that point? Check consideration, capacity, authority, any required formalities and subsequent variations. Read the signed terms, order confirmation and communications together. For sales of goods, check Part 3 of the *Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017* and whether the consumer guarantees apply. [Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/5/en/latest/) |
| The buyer can cancel for breach or misrepresentation | Identify any contractual remedy and the effect of s 34. Check damages for inducing misrepresentation under s 35, repudiation under s 36, and the essentiality or substantial-effect test under s 37. Check whether the party affirmed the contract with full knowledge under s 38 and when communication or other action made the cancellation effective under s 41. [Contract and Commercial Law Act 2017, Part 2, subpart 3](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2017/5/en/latest/) |
| The supplier must put a fault right | Apply the definitions of consumer and supplier in s 2, including the ordinary use of the goods or services and exclusions for particular purposes. Where parties in trade agreed to contract out, check every condition in s 43, including writing and whether the agreement was fair and reasonable. For goods, identify the guarantee and supplier remedy in Parts 1–2. For services, check ss 28–32 and the cancellation provisions in ss 35–38. Claims against manufacturers have their own grounds and remedies in Part 3. [Consumer Guarantees Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/0091/latest/contents.html) |
| An advertisement misled the customer | Preserve the wording, images, qualifications, audience and transaction date. Check conduct in trade under s 9 and any specific representation within s 13. If the customer seeks money, identify the loss, its connection to the conduct and the order available under s 43. A challenge to a standard-form term raises separate questions about coverage and who can seek a declaration under ss 46H–46I. [Fair Trading Act 1986, s 9](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/121/en/latest/sections/DLM96903/) · [Section 13](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/121/en/latest/sections/DLM96908/) · [Unfair contract terms](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/0121/latest/DLM6156643.html) |

**Worked citation check.** A household buys a new kettle from a shop. The original answer cites only *Consumer Guarantees Act 1993*, s 6, for “any fault gives the buyer an immediate refund”. Section 6 establishes a guarantee of acceptable quality. Section 7 supplies the test, including what a reasonable consumer would regard as acceptable given the goods, price and other circumstances. The original citation supports the guarantee and leaves the claimed remedy unsupported. [Consumer Guarantees Act 1993, ss 6–7](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/0091/latest/contents.html)

Record ss 16–23 as supplementary authorities. If the kettle has a readily fixable indicator fault and the failure is minor, s 18(2) lets the buyer require the shop to put it right within a reasonable time. Section 19 allows the supplier to choose among the applicable means of doing so. If the supplier refuses, neglects or fails to remedy it in time, s 18(2)(b) provides further options. If an electrical fault makes the kettle unsafe, s 21(d) treats the failure as substantial and s 18(3) permits rejection. Check loss of the right to reject under s 20 and notification and return under s 22. After valid rejection, s 23 gives the buyer the choice of a refund or a same-type, similar-value replacement if reasonably available in the supplier's stock. The answer therefore turns on the nature of the fault, the seller's response and the timing of rejection. [Consumer Guarantees Act 1993, Part 2](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/0091/latest/contents.html) · [Consumer Protection guidance on faulty goods](https://www.consumerprotection.govt.nz/general-help/common-consumer-issues/faulty-and-unsafe-products)
