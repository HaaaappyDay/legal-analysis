# Equity, trusts, business and information rights

Identify who owns the asset, who incurred the obligation, in what capacity each person acted and the remedy sought. A company register entry, a trust deed, a financing statement and a trade mark registration establish different facts. Record the date and scope of any register search.

| Issue | Questions and authorities |
|---|---|
| Equity and trusts | Obtain the deed and variations, identify the trust property and distinguish the powers of each settlor, trustee and beneficiary. For express trusts, check the mandatory duties in ss 22–27 and which default duties under ss 28–38 the terms modify or exclude. For fiduciary relief, identify the duty, any consent, the gain or loss and the basis for an account of profits, compensation or recovery of property. A tracing claim requires evidence following the property into the hands of the defendant. [Trusts Act 2019](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2019/38/en/latest/) |
| Restitution for a mistaken payment | Who paid whom, what did the payer believe, and would the payer otherwise have paid? Check whether a debt or settlement justified the transfer. For a mistake of law, apply s 74A of the *Property Law Act 2007*. Under s 74B, identify any good-faith change of position in reliance on the payment and whether full or partial recovery would be inequitable, including the effects on third parties. [Property Law Act 2007, ss 74A–74B](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2007/0091/latest/DLM969141.html) |
| Companies and directors | Check separate corporate personality under s 15, authority and execution under s 180, any personal guarantee, and who can enforce the obligation. For directors, identify the conduct and the particular duty, including good faith under s 131, reckless trading under s 135 and incurring obligations under s 136. *Yan v Mainzeal Property and Construction Ltd (in liq)* [2023] NZSC 113 at [269]–[273] explains how directors should approach ss 135 and 136. For the measure of loss, follow the Court's separate analysis at [277]–[296]. [Companies Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/105/en/latest/) · [Supreme Court judgment](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2023/2023-NZSC-113.pdf) |
| Insolvency and security | Identify whether the debtor is a company in liquidation under Part 16 of the *Companies Act 1993* or an individual facing bankruptcy or another personal insolvency procedure. For competing claims over personal property, obtain the security agreements and registration records. Check enforceability, attachment and perfection under ss 36, 40 and 41 of the *Personal Property Securities Act 1999*, then test any special priority before applying the residual rule in s 66. [Companies Act 1993](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1993/105/en/latest/) · [Insolvency Act 2006](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2006/55/en/latest/) · [Personal Property Securities Act 1999](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1999/126/en/latest/) |
| Consumer credit | Identify the debtor, lender, purpose of the borrowing, and credit terms. Check the conditions in s 11 for a consumer credit contract, the lender responsibility principles in s 9C, initial disclosure in s 17 and the remedy for the particular breach. Use the version applicable when the agreement was made or varied. [Credit Contracts and Consumer Finance Act 2003](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2003/52/en/latest/) |
| Insurance and financial services | For a declined claim, obtain the policy wording, application, renewal or variation, loss date and insurer’s reasons. Before applying a disclosure duty under the *Contracts of Insurance Act 2024*, check s 2, any commencement orders for the provision and Schedule 1 for its application to that contract or variation. For misleading financial product or service sales, check s 19 of the *Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013*. [Contracts of Insurance Act 2024 as enacted, s 2 and Schedule 1](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2024/46/en/2024-11-15.pdf) · [Financial Markets Conduct Act 2013, Part 2](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2013/0069/latest/DLM4091534.html) |
| Competition | Identify the product and geographic market, the relationship between participants and the conduct. Check arrangements affecting competition under s 27, cartel provisions and exceptions under ss 30–33, misuse of market power under s 36 and acquisitions under s 47. Match the evidence and any clearance or authorisation to the statutory test for the conduct in question. [Commerce Act 1986](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1986/5/en/latest/) |
| Privacy | Identify the agency, the information and what happened to it. Apply the relevant information privacy principle in s 22 and any applicable code. Distinguish access or correction requests, a complaint alleging interference with privacy, and notification after a breach. For notification, check serious harm, who must be notified and the exceptions in ss 112–116. [Privacy Act 2020, s 22](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/0031/latest/LMS151131.html) · [Privacy Act 2020, Part 6](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2020/31/en/latest/sections/LMS23504/) |
| Intellectual property | Identify the subject matter, owner, territory, term and allegedly infringing act. For copyright check protected works, restricted acts and first ownership under ss 14, 16 and 21, then any assignment, licence or exception. For a registered mark, compare the sign, goods or services and use in trade under s 89. For patents, read the claims and check the grant. For registered designs, identify the article and scope of the registration under s 11. [Copyright Act 1994](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1994/143/en/latest/) · [Trade Marks Act 2002, s 89](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2002/49/en/latest/sections/DLM164679/) · [Patents Act 2013](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2013/0068/latest/whole.html) · [Designs Act 1953, s 11](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1953/65/en/latest/sections/DLM281725/) |

**Worked citation check.** A beneficiary asks for the trustee's correspondence about a distribution. An answer cites s 51 of the *Trusts Act 2019* and promises every email. Section 51 establishes a presumption about notifying beneficiaries of basic trust information. For this request, also check what counts as trust information under s 49, including the exclusion for reasons for trustees' decisions. Apply the s 52 disclosure presumption and the s 53 factors, including confidentiality and the interests of other beneficiaries. The content of the correspondence and any conditions on disclosure matter to the result. [Trusts Act 2019, Part 3, subpart 3](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2019/38/en/latest/)
