# Criminal law, evidence, civil proceedings and enforcement

Identify the charge, claim or order under challenge, the remedy sought and the procedural stage. Record when the conduct occurred and when proceedings were filed, served or decided. These dates can select different versions of substantive and procedural law. [Court structure and appeal routes](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/about-the-judiciary/structure-of-the-court-system)

| Question | What decides it? |
|---|---|
| What must the prosecution establish, and where will the case be tried? | Read the provision creating the charged offence, including its elements, exceptions, penalty and version on the date of the alleged conduct. For procedure, apply the offence categories in s 6 and Schedule 1 of the Criminal Procedure Act 2011, then check the applicable rules for election of jury trial, place of trial and appeal. [Criminal Procedure Act 2011, s 6](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2011/81/en/latest/sections/DLM3360039/) |
| Can the defendant obtain bail, or what sentence can the court impose? | Identify whether the defendant awaits trial, sentence or appeal. For bail, check entitlement, restrictions and the tests for that stage, including ss 7–8 and 13 of the Bail Act. For sentencing, check the penalty provision and the purposes and principles in ss 7–8 of the Sentencing Act alongside the facts and relevant sentencing decisions. [Bail Act 2000](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2000/38/en/latest/) · [Sentencing Act 2002, ss 7–8](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2002/9/en/2025-06-17/) |
| Which civil forum can make the order sought? | For the Disputes Tribunal, check the nature and value of the disputed claim, exclusions and available orders under ss 10, 11 and 19. If proceedings are already before the Tribunal, look separately at rehearing (s 49) and the restricted appeal grounds (s 50). For the District Court check ss 74–76 and any specialist jurisdiction. Check limitation defences, including when a money claim was filed and any relevant late knowledge under ss 11–14 of the Limitation Act. [Disputes Tribunal Act 1988](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1988/110/en/latest/) · [District Court Act 2016, ss 74–76](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2016/49/en/2026-02-17.pdf) · [Limitation Act 2010](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2010/110/en/latest/) |
| Can a New Zealand proceeding be served overseas, or an Australian judgment enforced here? | Identify where service will take place and which rules govern the proceeding. In a High Court case check rr 6.27–6.28 and any applicable special regime. For Australian service, forum challenges and registration in New Zealand, follow the relevant provisions of the Trans-Tasman Proceedings Act. For registration, check the issuing body, type and finality of judgment, exclusions and the registration and notification steps. [High Court Rules 2016, pt 6](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/secondary-legislation/pco-drafted/2016/225/en/2026-01-01.pdf) · [Trans-Tasman Proceedings Act 2010, pt 2 subpts 1, 2 and 5](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2010/0108/latest/DLM2576223.html) |
| Can a party set aside or enforce an arbitral award? | Check the seat, arbitration agreement and court asked to act. The Arbitration Act deals separately with an application to set aside under sch 1 art 34 and recognition, enforcement and grounds for refusal under arts 35–36. For consumer contracts, also examine s 11. [Arbitration Act 1996](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1996/99/en/latest/) |

**Citation check.** An answer cites Evidence Act 2006, s 30(5) and says that evidence from an unlawful search must be excluded. Subsection (5) helps identify improperly obtained evidence. In criminal proceedings involving prosecution evidence, s 30(1)–(4) also requires a finding about how the evidence was obtained and an assessment of whether exclusion is proportionate. A reviewer needs the specific breach, its connection to the evidence and the circumstances relevant to that assessment before concluding the evidence must be excluded. [Evidence Act 2006, s 30](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2006/69/en/latest/sections/DLM393610/)
