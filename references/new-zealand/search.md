# Searching New Zealand legislation and judgments

## Find the provision as it stood on the relevant date

Open the supplied full-text link first. On New Zealand Legislation, check the title, document type, version date, section and subsection. For an Act or Parliamentary Counsel Office (PCO) instrument, use **Versions** to select the text covering the event. Check commencement, amendment notes and transitional provisions, including any in a Schedule. For an order, rule or notice, follow the empowering provision to the instrument. Agency-published instruments may require a visit to the issuing body's website. For an older Act available as converted text and an official scan, use the scan to resolve a discrepancy. [PCO navigation guide](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/navigate-legislation/) · [Historical versions and scans](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/previous-versions-of-legislation/) · [Official status of versions](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/about-legislation-status)

**Worked example — a claim lodged before the Disputes Tribunal limit increased.** A claimant lodged a $40,000 claim before 24 January 2026 and abandoned $10,000 to bring it within the former limit. The claim was still pending on that date. The amendment raised the s 10(3) ceiling from $30,000 to $60,000, but Schedule 1AA cl 3 of the principal Act deals specifically with earlier claims where an excess was abandoned. It prevents amending the pending claim to restore that amount or withdrawing or seeking strike-out to refile for it. Ask when the claim was lodged and whether any excess was abandoned. The hearing date and the new limit alone cannot answer the question. [Disputes Tribunal Amendment Act 2025, ss 2, 4, 7 and sch 1](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/2025/60/en/2025-10-24B.pdf) · [Disputes Tribunal Act 1988 as at 24 January 2026, s 10 and sch 1AA cl 3](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1988/110/en/2026-01-24.pdf)

If a section URL opens only an error or a search interface, search the official domain by title, distinctive wording and date. For the example above, try `site:legislation.govt.nz "Disputes Tribunal" "abandons" "$30,000"`. Open the dated full Act or official PDF and find the provision in context. For a Chinese-language query, translate the legal issue into local search terms. For “小额索赔能否上诉”, try `"Disputes Tribunal Act 1988" "appeal" "unfair"` and compare the provisions for a rehearing and an appeal. If only an index entry or snippet is readable, record the source as located and identify the text still to be verified. [Disputes Tribunal Act 1988, ss 49–50](https://www.legislation.govt.nz/act/public/1988/110/en/latest/)

## Find a judgment and trace its treatment

| Starting point | How to reach the reasons |
|---|---|
| A neutral citation such as `[2021] NZSC 85` | Search the exact citation and party names. NZSC, NZCA and NZHC identify the Supreme Court, Court of Appeal and High Court. On a Courts of New Zealand case page, choose **Decision**. A leave judgment, media release and substantive reasons can all concern the same parties. [Judicial Decisions Online](https://www.justice.govt.nz/courts/decisions/jdo/) · [Example case page](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/cases/bathurst-resources-limited-v-l-m-coal-holdings-limited) |
| A report citation, topic or specialist decision | Search party names, NZLR volume and page, or the statutory section plus the decisive facts. For a tribunal use its own decision collection or the Ministry's finder. Judicial Decisions Online covers the High Court from 2005, the Court of Appeal from 2003 and the Supreme Court, subject to publication restrictions. For a missing decision, check the court's collection or registry and state the limits of the material obtained. [Courts' access guide](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/going-to-court/media/finding-out-about-a-case) · [Ministry of Justice document finders](https://www.justice.govt.nz/about/document-finders/) |
| A claim that a precedent still governs | Trace appeals by party name, then search the neutral and report citations alongside the specific proposition. Open later judgments, identify the citing paragraph, and check whether it applies, qualifies, distinguishes or merely mentions the earlier reasons. For a statutory holding, also check later amendments. Record the databases and period actually searched. |

For an older reported judgment, retain the NZLR citation and the pinpoint page if the reasons have no numbered paragraphs. NZLII can help locate the decision by party name. Check the report or court copy before presenting a database-assigned identifier as the court's neutral citation. [New Zealand Law Style Guide, rule 3.2](https://lawfoundation.org.nz/style-guide2019/chapter-pt.3.2.html)

**Reading separate reasons.** In *Bathurst Resources Ltd v L & M Coal Holdings Ltd* the Court allowed the appeal over a payment under a coal transaction. For the use of extrinsic evidence in interpreting a contract, [50]–[53] record counsel's submissions. Read the judges' analysis beginning at [54] and the other judges' agreement at \[232](a). When applying it to a negotiation email, identify the disputed words and what the email could establish about their objective meaning. [Supreme Court judgment, [2021] NZSC 85](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2021/2021-NZSC-85.pdf)

**Checking a later citation.** In *MB Technology Ltd v Orbis Blockchain Technologies Ltd*, [129] and footnote 86 cite *Bathurst* on the objective approach. The issue at [129]–[130] was whether the parties had agreed to a collateral contract. That passage illustrates a later use of *Bathurst*. It supplies no general holding that every negotiation email is admissible when interpreting an existing contract. [High Court judgment, [2025] NZHC 1012](https://www.courtsofnz.govt.nz/assets/cases/2025/2025-NZHC-1012.pdf)
