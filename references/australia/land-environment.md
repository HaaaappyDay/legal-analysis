# Indigenous land rights, cultural heritage, environment and planning

A single parcel of land may involve multiple rights and permits. Record the land and waters concerned, tenure history, proposed activities, decision-making body and relevant dates, and assess each issue separately using the table below.

| What the user is actually asking | What to verify |
|---|---|
| Who holds native title and what it comprises | Using the *Native Title Act 1993*, applicable cases, existing determinations and their maps and schedules of rights, check the group, geographical area, traditional connection and historical grants of tenure. Registration of a claim, a court determination and authorisation of a particular activity have different legal effects. [Native Title Act 1993](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04665/latest/details) |
| How a project affects Indigenous rights or cultural heritage | For a future act, identify the category of act, applicable procedures and agreements, and verify whether a registered Indigenous Land Use Agreement exists and which parties and parcels it covers. Examine state or territory land rights legislation, cultural heritage protection and permissions affecting protected places separately; the absence of a native title determination does not itself resolve heritage protection issues. [National Native Title Tribunal on future acts](https://www.nntt.gov.au/futureacts) · [State and territory legislation](https://www.opc.gov.au/links/legislation-states-territories-and-new-zealand) |
| Whether construction can commence | Check zoning, local planning instruments, development consent conditions and duration, state environmental permits and whether federal approval is triggered. Distinguish submission of a referral, assessment, formal approval and satisfaction of preconditions. When consulting legislation, check the transitional arrangements for reforms against the project date. [Federal Environment Protection and Biodiversity Conservation Act and versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00485/latest/details) |
| Mining, water use, energy facilities or environmental enforcement | Check the subject matter and boundaries of mining rights and exploration or production licences; separately examine rights of access to land, water rights, emissions conditions and environmental orders. Review administrative penalties, remediation orders, civil damages and criminal liability against their respective authorities; state legislation and the actual licence conditions may determine the outcome. [State and territory legislation databases](https://www.opc.gov.au/links/legislation-states-territories-and-new-zealand) |

**Citation verification example.** “The company has obtained a state mining licence, so it can enter the entire area and begin work.” The original citation supports only the authority actually specified in the licence. For the affected parcels, also examine access rights, planning and environmental conditions, and future act procedures; identify separately which steps have been completed and which still lack decisive facts.
