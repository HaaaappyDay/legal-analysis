# Employment, industrial relations, workplace safety and workers' compensation

**Western Australian example.** If a user says, “I was dismissed in Perth”, the actual employer named in the employment contract and its legal structure still need to be checked. If the employer is a sole trader operating in Western Australia, begin with the Western Australian industrial relations system; if the employer is covered by the national system, verify the federal avenues instead. Where only the city of employment is given, explain these two possibilities while reserving a conclusion on the application body and applicable time limit. [Fair Work Ombudsman explanation of coverage, Western Australia section](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/fair-work-system)

| Matter | Distinctions to make when verifying citations |
|---|---|
| Pay, employment conditions and dismissal | Check the workplace location, the employer's legal structure, the nature of employment, the applicable workplace relations system, and any award or enterprise agreement. For unfair dismissal, assess eligibility, whether a dismissal occurred, its effective date, the application time limit and any extension separately. Other general protections or contractual claims require their own legal authorities. [Fair Work Act 2009 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/details) · [Fair Work Ombudsman explanation of coverage](https://www.fairwork.gov.au/about-us/workplace-laws/fair-work-system) |
| Workplace safety, workers' compensation and equality protections | Examine the regulatory jurisdiction, the party responsible for controlling the risk, the relevant industry and the locally adopted legislation. Workplace safety duties, statutory workers' compensation benefits, discrimination and adverse action each have their own conditions, time limits and responsible bodies; consult the local workers' compensation scheme and relevant federal or state anti-discrimination legislation. [Safe Work Australia on model laws and local adoption](https://www.safeworkaustralia.gov.au/law-and-regulation/model-whs-laws) |
| Collective bargaining, unions and industrial action | Establish whether the dispute concerns enterprise bargaining, agreement approval, union rights, workplace representation rights or protected industrial action. Check the authorised parties, procedures, covered groups and orders separately. Coverage by an enterprise agreement and legal protection for a particular action require independent authority. [Fair Work Act 2009](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/details) |

Where dismissal also involves discrimination, consult [human rights and anti-discrimination](government-rights.md) to check complaint avenues, restrictions on concurrent proceedings and the remedies sought. For benefits or NDIS support following injury, consult [social security](social-services.md); for superannuation contributions, see [tax and superannuation](tax-super.md).

**Citation verification example.** The user provides FWC guidance on time limits, and the original answer states that “time starts when the dismissal notice is received, and there is no remedy if the deadline is missed”. Check *Fair Work Act* s 394(2)–(3), distinguishing the notice date, the effective date of dismissal and the filing date, and extensions in exceptional circumstances. If coverage by the national system, the period of employment or the nature of the dismissal remains unclear, retain the relevant qualifications; the provision supports analysis of the time limit, while prospects on the merits require other authorities. [Fair Work Act 2009](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2009A00028/latest/details) · [FWC guidance on deadlines](https://www.fwc.gov.au/apply-or-lodge/deadlines)
