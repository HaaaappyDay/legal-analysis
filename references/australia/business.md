# Companies, finance, competition, intellectual property and privacy

Identify the status, place of registration, authority and industry of the entities and counterparties involved in the transaction, and the relevant dates. Companies, directors, shareholders, trustees and sole traders may owe different obligations; assess civil claims, investigations by regulators such as ASIC, and criminal liability separately.

| Issue | Verification method |
|---|---|
| Execution by companies and third parties | Consult *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth) ss 126–129, addressing separately an agent's authority, methods of execution by a company, the assumptions available to third parties and their limits. Check the company's director structure at the time of execution, the capacity in which each person signed, whether the company was a party to the transaction, and the third party's knowledge. Consult the specific provisions on financial assistance and related party transactions separately. [Corporations Act and versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/details) |
| Partnerships and other business structures | Examine the business arrangements, partnership agreement, authority to deal with third parties, dates of admission or retirement, and applicable state law. Verify separately the effects of registering a business name, incorporating a company and forming a limited liability partnership. [Australian Government guide to partnerships](https://business.gov.au/planning/business-structures-and-types/business-structures/partnership) |
| Directors, shareholders and insolvency | For directors' duties, consult ss 180–184 and other relevant provisions, checking the duty holder, conduct, conflicts of interest, defences and remedies individually. Corporate external administration, liquidation and personal bankruptcy involve different entities and procedures; the existence and registration of security may affect creditors' priority. [Corporations Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/details) · [Bankruptcy Act 1966 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1966A00033/latest/details) |
| Financial products, credit, insurance and competition | Check the nature of the product, the licensed entity, the customer category and the regulator. For misleading conduct, unfair terms and dispute resolution in financial services, check sector-specific rules such as the ASIC Act; assess competition issues against the relevant parts of the *Competition and Consumer Act*, the market definition and the conduct. [ASIC financial services](https://www.asic.gov.au/regulatory-resources/financial-services) · [Competition and Consumer Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/details) |
| Personal property securities and priority | Check the collateral, debtor, creation of the security interest, registration or other means of perfection, timing and exceptions to priority rules. A register search can establish registration status; a claim to priority over another creditor also requires examination of both parties' rights and the PPSA priority rules. [PPSR guide to priority](https://www.ppsr.gov.au/managing-and-maintaining/enforce-your-registration/which-security-interest-has-priority) |
| Trade marks, patents, designs and copyright | Check registration, duration, ownership, territory, infringing conduct and exceptions separately for each type of right. Business names, domain names and overseas registration records support only what they actually record. Record the search date when consulting a rights register. [IP Australia guide to types of rights](https://ipfirstresponse.ipaustralia.gov.au/options/what-ip-and-how-does-it-protect-me) |
| Privacy, data and personality interests | First identify the organisation, information or infringement concerned and the remedy sought. For APP entity coverage, exceptions, information use and complaint avenues, consult the *Privacy Act 1988* and the APPs; for the statutory tort for serious invasions of privacy, consult Schedule 2 and its commencement date separately. Check local laws separately for state public sector bodies, health records and surveillance. [Privacy Act 1988](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03712/latest/details) · [OAIC explanation of the statutory tort](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/more-privacy-rights/statutory-tort-for-serious-invasions-of-privacy) |
| Online content and platform regulation | Identify whether the user seeks content removal, a complaint to the platform, intervention by eSafety, or a claim based on defamation, privacy or copyright. Check the type of harm, the platform, prerequisites for a complaint and statutory thresholds; eligibility to complain does not establish an entitlement to compensation. [Online Safety Act 2021](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2021A00076/latest) · [eSafety guide to reporting adult cyber abuse](https://www.esafety.gov.au/key-topics/adult-cyber-abuse/report-adult-cyber-abuse) |

**Citation verification example.** The original answer cites s 127 and states that “a company cannot be bound by a document that was not executed in accordance with that section”. After verifying the methods of execution addressed by s 127, also examine s 126 and the applicable rules of agency. If the counterparty also relies on statutory assumptions, explain the relevant conditions in ss 128–129 and the available facts. Record the missing facts about authority and the overgeneralisation of the original citation separately in the report. [Corporations Act 2001, Part 2B.2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00818/latest/details)
