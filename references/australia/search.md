# Searching Australian legislation and judgments

## If a full-text link is available, read it directly with fetch first

Pass the official full-text URL supplied by the user directly to fetch, verify the jurisdiction, version or identity of the judgment, then locate the relevant provisions or paragraphs. The materials below illustrate how to locate text; accessibility and the content actually retrieved depend on the tool results in the current session.

| Location example | What to verify when reading, and limits |
|---|---|
| [Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth), Schedule 2, Federal Register HTML](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2025-02-21/2025-02-21/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_4/document_4.html) | Verify the 21 February 2025 version and s 260(1), and locate the conditions under which a failure of goods to comply with a guarantee constitutes a major failure. This link illustrates historical version retrieval only; assessing refund rights also requires reading the relevant remedies provisions and separately verifying the version applicable to the question. |
| [ACCC v TPG Internet Pty Ltd [2013] HCA 54, High Court judgment PDF](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2013/HCA/54.pdf) | Locate paras 45–53 and check the majority's analysis of the advertisements' dominant message and presentation. Examine later cases separately if the continuing authority of the decision needs to be assessed. |

## If a link is missing or direct retrieval fails, locate the text with search

For federal legislation, search `site:legislation.gov.au "Competition and Consumer Act 2010" "259" "260" 2025-02-21`. If only a contents page or latest/details page is retrieved, continue looking for the dated full text and corresponding volume. For judgments, search `site:hcourt.gov.au "TPG" "[2013] HCA 54"`, using the case name and `judgment PDF` to locate the reasons; if only catchwords, a summary or a party's submissions are available, identify the material type.

**Historical NSW legislation: an alternative when direct retrieval fails.** If direct retrieval of the required HTML or PDF fails in the current session, search by Act title, issue and applicable date. For example, to find tenancy rules in April 2025, search `site:legislation.nsw.gov.au "Residential Tenancies Act 2010" "urgent repairs" April 2025`. If the tool supplies an indexed copy of the official PDF, open it, find `Landlord’s general obligation` and `Urgent repairs to residential premises` in the text, and check the version period in the footer. Record a particular scope as verified only when the document's identity, continuous text and corresponding version have actually been read; readable search results do not guarantee that other retrieval methods will work for the same URL. If only a summary is available, record “located; original text not verified”; state government procedural guidance may be listed separately as explanatory material. [Historical version location example](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2025-04-25/act-2010-042)

**Later cases.** Try citators available in the current environment, including LawCite; availability depends on the current access result. If database access fails or the required search functionality is unavailable, search for subsequent judgments by neutral citation, case name and issue, then open the courts' reasons to check how they treated the original case. The first result may be a party's submissions, so inspect the document heading. Report verification results only for later cases actually read.

Past successful retrievals and failures are recorded separately in [historical access observations](../search-observations.md), solely as troubleshooting references. Do not use them to presume a database is unavailable or treat earlier verification as verification in the current session.

## Find provisions and the applicable version

1. **Parse the citation.** For example, in `Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (Cth) sch 2 s 259(3)`, Cth identifies the Commonwealth, sch 2 identifies Schedule 2, and the final component identifies the provision. Search federal legislation by its official title in the Federal Register of Legislation, selecting the point-in-time version or compilation by the date of the facts; open the relevant volume, Schedule and subsection, and check commencement, application and transitional provisions. For identifiers such as NSW or Vic, use the local legislation database to find historical versions; for uniform legislation, also check local adoption and modifications. [Federal Register search help](https://www.legislation.gov.au/help-and-resources/using-the-legislation-register/how-to-use-browse-search-and-advanced-search) · [State and territory legislation entry points](https://www.opc.gov.au/links/legislation-states-territories-and-new-zealand)
2. **If the question is supplied only in Chinese, convert it into local legal terminology.** For example, for “do defective goods always entitle the buyer to a refund?”, search `Australian Consumer Law goods "major failure" supplier refund`. Return from the results to Schedule 2 and separately verify the content of the guarantee (such as s 54), remedies against suppliers (s 259), major failure (s 260), and restrictions on and consequences of rejection (ss 262–263). Retain the English Act title, jurisdiction, version and specific provisions in the report. The historical text below illustrates how to locate a version. [21 February 2025 version, Schedule 2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/2025-02-21/2025-02-21/text/original/epub/OEBPS/document_4/document_4.html)

## Find judgments and subsequent treatment

| Available lead | Search approach |
|---|---|
| A neutral citation | For example, for `[2013] HCA 54`, begin with the complete citation or case name in High Court Judgments; for FCA or FCAFC, use Federal Court Judgments; for NSWCA, use NSW Caselaw. Follow the case page to the reasons for judgment and verify the court, date, authors, paragraphs and final orders. Case progress pages and counsel's submissions may help locate the case, but cannot by themselves establish that the court accepted an argument. [High Court Judgments](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments) · [Federal Court Judgments](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/digital-law-library/judgments) · [NSW Caselaw](https://www.caselaw.nsw.gov.au/) |
| Only a case name, authorised report volume and page, or an issue | Search by one or both parties' names, year, court, or volume and page. When searching by issue, filter with local legal terminology, section numbers and decisive facts, for example `"dominant message" broadband misleading advertising`. Older cases may have only report volume and page references; retain pinpoint page citations. Use AustLII to discover candidates across databases, then verify them against the court text or authorised report. [Federal Court citation guidance](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/law-and-practice/practice-documents/practice-notes/gpn-auth) · [AustLII](https://www.austlii.edu.au/) |
| A need to establish what the judgment still supports | Search subsequent appeals and judgments citing the case by neutral citation, report citation and case name; if an interactive citator is available, also search citing cases. Open later cases and distinguish affirmation, overruling, limitation, distinguishing and mere mention; then check whether the relevant provisions have been amended. Case records can confirm procedural developments, but subsequent legal treatment should be located in the reasons. Qualify any subsequent status that has not yet been verified. [Federal Court judgments search FAQ](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/digital-law-library/judgments/judgments-faq) · [University of Sydney guide to legal sources](https://www.library.sydney.edu.au/support/searching/understanding-legal-sources) |

**Sample search.** Search `"[2013] HCA 54" TPG` and open the full judgment in *ACCC v TPG Internet Pty Ltd*. Paragraphs 45–53 contain the majority's analysis of the overall impression of the advertisements and their presentation. If the original citation consists only of the catchwords on the case page, use these paragraphs to verify the answer; keywords alone also cannot make the case support a conclusion about consumers' rights to return goods for a refund. [High Court case page](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments/judgments-1998-current/australian-competition-and-consumer-commission-v-tpg-internet-pty-ltd) · [Full judgment](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2013/HCA/54.pdf)

If a court website search yields no results, try another court, a different spelling of the case name, an older report citation or another database. Record which materials were actually read; if only a headnote, summary or party's materials were available, state the corresponding limitations. The absence of new citations in a database does not establish that the decision has consistently been followed since it was handed down.
