# Contracts, consumer guarantees and misleading conduct

First identify the parties, goods or services, purpose of acquisition, method and time of supply, contract terms and remedy sought. Distinguish contractual terms, common law and equitable rules, the Australian Consumer Law (ACL), and the legislation applying it in the relevant state or territory. The ACL is in Schedule 2 to the federal *Competition and Consumer Act 2010*; specify the Schedule when citing it and select the version applicable at the time of the transaction. [Act and versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/details)

| Proposition in the answer | Questions to verify |
|---|---|
| A contract was formed or the other party breached an obligation | Check offer, acceptance, consideration or execution as a deed, intention to create legal relations, incorporation of terms and the authority of representatives. Find separate authority for express terms, implied obligations and statutory guarantees. For disputed terms, review the timing of notice, the text, the transactional context and applicable legislation. |
| A breach permits termination or a claim for a specified amount | Identify the termination clause, the nature and consequences of the breach, notice requirements and time for performance; check causation, foreseeability and mitigation when calculating damages. For a stipulated contractual sum, also examine penalty cases and the purpose of the term. If bank fee cases are cited, check the majority reasons and the context of the transaction in question. [Paciocco v ANZ [2016] HCA 28](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/cases-and-judgments/judgments/judgments-1998-current/paciocco-v-australia-and-new-zealand-banking-group-limited) |
| A defective product entitles the buyer to an immediate refund | Check the conditions for application of ACL ss 3, 54 and other relevant provisions, and the specific guarantee. For remedies against suppliers, consult ss 259–263, including whether the failure can be remedied, whether it is major, restrictions on the right to reject, and who chooses the remedy. For services, consult ss 267–269 separately. Expiry of a manufacturer's voluntary warranty does not itself determine whether a statutory guarantee has been breached. [ACL Act and historical versions](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/details) · [ACCC explanation of repairs, replacements and refunds](https://www.accc.gov.au/consumers/problem-with-a-product-or-service-you-bought/repair-replace-refund-cancel) |
| Advertising, standard form terms or platform conduct is unlawful | Consult ACL s 18, s 29, ss 23–28 or other provisions according to the claim made; coverage, the requirement of conduct in trade or commerce, liability and remedies may differ. Private claims, regulatory enforcement and civil penalties each require their own authority. |

**Using cases.** *ACCC v TPG Internet Pty Ltd* [2013] HCA 54 concerned the advertised price of broadband and conditions requiring bundled services. Paragraphs 45–53 of the majority reasons discuss the impression formed by the audience on encountering the advertisements and their presentation. If the original answer states that “an advertisement cannot be misleading if the conditions are disclosed in fine print”, these reasons can be used to test that absolute wording. Compare the audience and the prominence of the headline and additional conditions, and check the statutory versions applicable to the different advertisements in the case. [Full High Court judgment](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2013/HCA/54.pdf)

**Citation verification example.** The original answer cites only s 54 and states that “any quality problem entitles a customer to return the goods immediately for a refund”. Section 54 can support the premise that a quality guarantee applies. Identify ss 259, 260, 262 and 263 as supplementary authorities identified during review; after checking the severity of the problem, the seller's opportunity to remedy it and the conditions for rejection, specify the facts under which a refund may be chosen. Even if a right to a refund ultimately exists, separately assess the extent to which the original single-section citation supports the answer. [ACL Schedule 2, Parts 3-2 and 5-4](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00109/latest/details)
