# Migration, refugee protection and citizenship

Verify separately the applicant's location and status at the relevant time, visa class or pathway to citizenship, application and decision dates, method of notification and remedy sought. Visa grant, work rights and eligibility for citizenship are distinct legal propositions.

| Question | Citation verification approach |
|---|---|
| Visa applications, conditions and cancellation | Consult the *Migration Act 1958*, *Migration Regulations 1994* and instruments applicable at the relevant time for the subclass, application validity, substantive criteria at the time of decision, visa conditions and cancellation powers. Policy webpages may explain documents and steps; locate specific grounds for refusal or cancellation in the legislation and decision notice. [Migration Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/details) · [Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/) |
| Refugees and protection | Check whether the claim concerns the refugee criterion or complementary protection, and whether the specific facts, risks, exclusions and exceptions correspond to the cited provision. For a protection visa question, begin with s 36 and the relevant definitions; assess general country information separately from the applicant's individual evidence. [Migration Act s 36](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/details) |
| Merits review and judicial review | Verify whether that type of decision is reviewable, who may apply, notification and time limit rules, and conditions concerning location or detention status. An ART application webpage can explain how to lodge an application, but may not establish jurisdiction over a particular decision. For judicial review, separately examine jurisdiction, reviewable errors and remedies. [ART immigration and citizenship matters](https://www.art.gov.au/applying-review/immigration-and-citizenship) |
| Citizenship | For the particular pathway, such as birth, descent or conferral, check status, parents' status, residence and absence dates, and applicable exceptions. After using a permanent visa confirmation letter to support residence status, also check the relevant conditions for acquiring citizenship. [Australian Citizenship Act 2007](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2007A00020/latest/details) |

**Citation verification example.** “Everyone has the same number of days to apply to the ART after a visa refusal letter is issued.” If the original citation is a webpage about a particular visa category, confine its scope to the decision types it specifies. Also check the decision notice, statutory review jurisdiction, service rules and time limit provisions then in force; if key dates are missing, state the conditions needed for the calculation.

**Case law | Will a court set aside a visa refusal whenever it contains a legal error?** In *Hossain*, the assessment of one visa criterion was erroneous, but failure to satisfy another criterion independently supported refusal. The High Court majority explained that whether an error is sufficient to affect the validity of a decision depends on the enabling legislation and its effect on the outcome. If the original answer goes further and requires the applicant to prove that “the visa would certainly have been granted if the error were corrected”, supplement it with *LPDT*'s explanation of materiality: where that threshold applies, the question is whether correction of the error could realistically have produced a different outcome. The review should identify which criterion or reason for decision was affected, and distinguish relief on judicial review from a fresh decision on the visa. [Hossain [2018] HCA 34, paras 29–31, 35–37](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2018/HCA/34.pdf) · [LPDT [2024] HCA 12, paras 14–16](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2024/HCA/12.pdf)
