# Property, residential tenancies, torts, family law and succession

First identify the location of the property, the type of relationship, the parties' capacities, and whether the user asks about entitlement, damages, registration, an application for orders or enforcement. The NSW examples below are for verifying questions concerning that state; for other states or territories, obtain the local rules from the [directory of official legislation databases](https://www.opc.gov.au/links/legislation-states-territories-and-new-zealand).

| Field | Facts and authorities to review |
|---|---|
| Land, title, security and trusts | Check registered title, the form of co-ownership, mortgages, priority and applicable state law; for equitable interests, check the conditions for their creation and their effect against third parties. Indigenous land rights issues also require verification of federal and local regimes according to the land, type of right and procedure involved. [Native Title Act 1993 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04665/latest/details) |
| Residential tenancies | First establish the state and living arrangements, and whether residential tenancy legislation applies or an exemption operates. Check the respective conditions for rent, bonds, repairs, termination notices and eviction orders. For NSW repair questions, consult *Residential Tenancies Act 2010* ss 62–65, distinguishing the scope of urgent repairs, notice and the costs of arranging repairs oneself, and applications to the tribunal for orders. Check the provisions and regulations then in force when determining amounts. [Example of searching and verifying historical NSW legislation](search.md) · [NSW housing authority guide to repairs](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/renting-a-place-to-live/getting-repairs-done) |
| Torts, injury and reputation | Consult the common law and relevant state legislation on duty of care, breach, causation, damage, defences, limitation periods and restrictions on costs. For NSW negligence propositions, begin with *Civil Liability Act 2002* ss 5B and 5D and check their scope of application. Defamation, motor accidents and workplace injuries require examination of specific legislation, statutory schemes and preliminary procedures. [Historical version of the NSW Civil Liability Act](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2024-08-16/act-2002-022) |
| Family relationships and property | Distinguish divorce, parenting arrangements, property orders, spousal maintenance, child support and local protection orders. Check marriage or de facto relationship status, geographical connections, separation dates and when divorce took effect. For de facto property matters in Western Australia, separately consult local law and court guidance; verify time limits and permission to apply out of time for each type of application. [Family Law Act 1975 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00275/latest/details) · [Family Court of Western Australia guide to de facto relationships](https://www.familycourt.wa.gov.au/D/defacto_relationships.aspx) |
| Wills, estate administration and family provision claims | Check the date of death, domicile, location of assets, testamentary formalities and capacity, grants of representation, eligible applicants and time limits. Establish whether an asset forms part of the estate; the form of joint ownership, superannuation death benefits and trusts require separate examination of the documents. When citing NSW *Succession Act 2006* s 58 to answer a time limit question, check the facts triggering the period and statutory exceptions. [Historical version of the NSW Succession Act](https://legislation.nsw.gov.au/view/whole/pdf/inforce/2025-08-25/act-2006-080) |

**Citation verification example.** A NSW tenant asks, “The hot water system has broken down. Can I arrange a repair and make the landlord pay?” The original answer cites the landlord's repair obligation. That citation can support the existence of the obligation; during review, add ss 62–64 and check whether the situation qualifies as urgent, notification or reasonable attempts to contact the landlord, an opportunity to address the problem, and the requirements concerning the repairer and costs. Include the nature of the fault and the notification history as conditions in the conclusion. [NSW guide to urgent repairs](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/rules/urgent-repairs-residential-rental-properties)

**The same question in a different state.** If the landlord lives in Sydney but the rented property is in Melbourne, begin with Victorian repair rules; the cited NSW webpage may accurately describe NSW practice yet be insufficient to answer the question about this property. If all that is known is that the property is in Australia, list the reimbursement cap and application avenue as matters still to be determined. Comparing the two authorities' guidance, the limits on the cost of arranging urgent repairs oneself, where the respective conditions are met, are A$1,000 in NSW and A$2,500 in Victoria. Before applying them to an individual case, check the event date and provisions then in force. [NSW repair guidance](https://www.nsw.gov.au/housing-and-construction/renting-a-place-to-live/getting-repairs-done) · [Victorian repair guidance](https://www.consumer.vic.gov.au/housing/renting/repairs-alterations-safety-and-pets/repairs/repairs-in-rental-properties)

**Two distinct interstate questions.** In a tort case, examine separately the place of the tort, the forum and any specific legislation; proceedings brought in the ACT may still be governed by NSW substantive tort law. For an estate spanning states, examine separately the deceased's domicile, the types and locations of assets, and where any existing grant was issued; if the question is “can an interstate grant be used to deal with NSW assets?”, consult local procedures for recognising or resealing an interstate grant. [John Pfeiffer v Rogerson [2000] HCA 36](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/sites/default/files/eresources/2000/HCA/36.pdf) · [Supreme Court of NSW guide to resealing](https://supremecourt.nsw.gov.au/wills-probate/applications/reseal-of-grant.html)
