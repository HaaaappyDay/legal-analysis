# Admiralty and maritime law, transport, customs and international trade

| Issue | Verification approach |
|---|---|
| Admiralty jurisdiction and ship arrest | Examine the categories of maritime claim in the *Admiralty Act 1988*, proceedings in personam or in rem, the relevant person liable, ship ownership at the relevant times and arrest procedures. Verify a claim for payment in a maritime dispute separately from the statutory conditions for arresting a particular ship. [Admiralty Act 1988](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03627/latest) · [Federal Court explanation of admiralty jurisdiction](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/law-and-practice/national-practice-areas/admiralty/jurisdiction) |
| Carriage of goods by sea, chartering and transport insurance | Verify the carrier, shipper, bill of lading or charterparty, ports of loading and discharge, voyage legs and the stage at which damage occurred. Using the *Carriage of Goods by Sea Act 1991*, applicable carriage rules and the contract, check the period of responsibility, liability limits, notice requirements, time bars and jurisdiction clauses. For insurance payments, separately examine insured risks, exclusions and notification of claims. Identify the applicable regimes separately for air and multimodal transport. [Federal Register of Legislation](https://www.legislation.gov.au/) |
| Tariffs, origin and import and export controls | Verify classification, value, origin, trade agreement treatment, declaration obligations and import permits separately. A commodity code alone does not establish that goods may enter the country; food, animals, plants and controlled goods require separate examination of biosecurity and import or export conditions. When using policy materials, identify the enabling legislation or instrument. [Australian Border Force tariff classification](https://www.abf.gov.au/importing-exporting-and-manufacturing/tariff-classification/overview) · [Biosecurity Act 2015](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2015A00061/latest) |

**Citation verification example.** “If a customer owes port service charges, any ship in the same corporate group can be arrested.” First establish during review which ship received the services and the type of claim, then examine the subject matter, person liable, ownership and sister ship conditions under *Admiralty Act* ss 15–19. Determine whether the original citation supports the debt or proceedings against a particular ship. [Admiralty Act 1988, Part III](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A03627/latest)
