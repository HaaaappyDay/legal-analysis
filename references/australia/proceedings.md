# Criminal law, dispute resolution and evidence

First record the cause of action or charge, procedural stage, court or tribunal, relevant documents and dates of service. State courts may exercise federal jurisdiction; explain the basis for selecting the substantive law, court rules, evidence law and appeal route separately. [Role of the High Court](https://www.hcourt.gov.au/about/overview-high-court/operation-high-court) · [State and territory legislation](https://www.opc.gov.au/links/legislation-states-territories-and-new-zealand)

| Issue | Facts and sources of law to check |
|---|---|
| Alleged offences, defences and penalties | First identify the Commonwealth offence or the specific state or territory offence. Check the time of the conduct, the physical and fault elements, statutory defences or exceptions, and burdens of proof. The federal *Criminal Code* is in the Schedule to the *Criminal Code Act 1995*; it cannot directly establish the elements of a state offence. Distinguish prosecution, conviction, sentencing and the statutory maximum penalty. [Federal Criminal Code Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04868/latest/details) |
| Criminal procedure and evidence | Check the relevant procedures separately for bail, evidence gathering, admissibility, jury directions, sentencing and appeals; where a federal case is heard in a state court, verify the mechanism by which procedural laws apply. Distinguish what the police allege, what the prosecution must prove, the standard for establishing facts on which admissibility depends, and the standard the court must apply to its findings. |
| Civil liability and standards of proof | Before citing the *Evidence Act 1995* (Cth), check its scope under s 4. Where the Act applies, s 140 concerns civil proof, s 141 distinguishes the statutory standards for the prosecution and defence cases, and s 142 separately addresses facts relevant to admissibility. Also identify who bears the evidential or persuasive burden; for other courts, consult the local evidence law. [Federal Evidence Act, ss 4, 140–142](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04858/latest/details) |
| Civil litigation, arbitration and enforcement | Check the proper plaintiffs and defendants, jurisdictional basis, state or federal court rules, procedural stage and limitation periods, distinguishing civil claims, remedies under specific legislation and tribunal procedures. For domestic commercial arbitration, check local arbitration legislation and the agreement; for international arbitration, examine federal law, the seat, enforcement and grounds for refusing recognition. [International Arbitration Act 1974 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00192/latest/details) |
| Appeals, leave and judicial review | Distinguish appeals from final judgments and interlocutory orders, whether leave is required, whether factual findings may be challenged, when time begins to run and the power to extend time. Practice guidance explains how to lodge an appeal; statutory appeal rights require separate examination of the enabling legislation and court rules. [Federal Court explanation of appellate jurisdiction](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/law-and-practice/appeals/from-courts/appellate-jurisdiction) |
| Cross-border disputes | Examine separately the law governing the issue, jurisdiction or arbitration clauses, service and evidence abroad, and recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments or arbitral awards. Verify treaty scope, implementing legislation and the countries or courts covered; a contractual choice of law directly answers only the relevant choice-of-law issue. |

**Citation verification example.** The user asks about the standard for a criminal conviction, but the original citation is s 140. After verifying the relevant court, explain that the cited provision concerns civil proceedings; add s 141(1) to explain the standard for the prosecution case. If the admissibility of particular evidence is disputed, also verify s 142 and the relevant admissibility provisions. Identify individually which propositions are correct but incorrectly cited and which propositions themselves require revision. [Evidence Act 1995 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A04858/latest/details)
