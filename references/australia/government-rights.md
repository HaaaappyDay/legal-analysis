# Constitutional law, administrative law, freedom of information and anti-discrimination

| Issue | Decisive facts and authorities |
|---|---|
| Federalism, legislative power and conflicting laws | Identify the legislature, the relevant head of Commonwealth power, the two specific obligations and the alleged conflict. When citing s 109 of the Australian Constitution, verify whether valid Commonwealth law is inconsistent with state law and the extent of any inconsistency; the text of that section refers to state laws. Examine the basis of territory legislation separately. [Australian Constitution ss 51, 109, 122](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004Q00685/latest) |
| Administrative decisions and judicial review | Identify the decision-maker, the governing legislation, whether statutory power was exercised, procedural fairness issues and the orders sought. For merits review, check whether the enabling legislation confers review jurisdiction; for judicial review, check the court, grounds of review, privative clauses and available remedies. For orders concerning Commonwealth officers, also verify Constitution s 75(v). [Constitution](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004Q00685/latest) · [ADJR Act 1977](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A01697/latest/details) |
| Access to government information | Check the agency and documents concerned, applicable Commonwealth or local freedom of information legislation, exemptions, conditional exemptions and public interest assessments, and avenues for internal or external review. If the person's own information is involved, also consult [privacy law](business.md). [Freedom of Information Act 1982 (Cth)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A02562/latest/details) |
| Human rights, discrimination and complaints | Identify the protected attribute, the activity involved, such as employment, education, accommodation or services, direct or indirect discrimination, statutory exceptions and proof. Consult the federal Racial, Sex, Disability or Age Discrimination Act and local legislation separately; when citing a human rights charter, check whether one has been enacted in the jurisdiction, whom it covers and the remedies available. Verify powers and time limits separately for complaints to a commission, conciliation, termination of a complaint and applications to a court. [Australian Human Rights Commission complaints guide](https://humanrights.gov.au/complaints/how-to-make-a-complaint) |

**Citation verification example.** “The commission accepted my complaint, so discrimination has been established.” The acknowledgement letter can support the procedural status. Identify the provisions covering the protected attribute and the alleged discriminatory conduct, any applicable exceptions, and how the complainant can obtain a determination. Specify which facts still need to be established. If the user asks whether they can sue, also check the stage of the complaints process and the conditions for applying to a court.
